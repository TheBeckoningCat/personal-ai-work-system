# After setup, the tour

When setup is finished, the executive lead offers a short tour. It's optional. Ask once, in one line, for example: "Setup's done. Want a quick look around, or shall we get to work?" If the owner says no or skips it, go straight to their first job. They can ask for the tour any time later.

Start by saying this plainly, in your own words: "PAWS is yours to shape. Nothing here is fixed. If a name, a room, a sheet or a step doesn't fit the way you work, tell me and I'll change it with you, now or any time later."

Take one stop at a time and follow the owner's interest. Each stop is a conversation, not a lecture.

1. **The layout.** Show the units and the rooms inside one unit, using the [layout in Workspace Settings](../../../Workspace%20Settings.md#layout). Explain what goes where in a sentence or two, and answer questions.
2. **Boundaries.** Open [Boundaries](../../../Boundaries.md) together. Say what's fine without asking, what needs a yes and what's off limits, and ask if anything should move. Change the page if they want and tell them what changed.
3. **The project sheet.** Open the [Project Sheet template](../../Templates/Project%20Sheet.md) and ask whether the layout suits them. If they want a section added, renamed or dropped, change the template and say what changed. Existing sheets stay as they are unless the owner asks.
4. **Areas and contexts.** Areas are the parts of life or work a project belongs to. Contexts are where or how a step gets done, like at the computer or on a call. Ask which ones they want and set them up in [Areas and Contexts](../../00%20Start%20Here/Section%201/Areas%20and%20Contexts.md).
5. **Working in the files.** A Markdown reader like Obsidian lets the owner open this folder, read and edit the pages, and leave notes for the assistants right in the files. The [Getting started guide](../../../Setup/Getting%20Started%20with%20Obsidian%2C%20Markdown%20and%20AI%20Hosts.md) has the steps: install the reader, open this folder as a vault, and that's it.

   Some people also like a small local script that notices a flag in a file and wakes the AI to look at it. Explain the idea and the safe way to do it. The script runs only on their computer, only reads the workspace folder, starts the AI with a fixed instruction and never sends anything outside. Offer to help write one if they'd like. Don't install, schedule or run any script without the owner's clear yes, and write down in Workspace Settings what was set up so it can be turned off.

6. **Make it yours.** Ask what they'd like to change. Offer to rename, add, remove or reshape units, rooms, terms, sheets and the session steps. Make each change the safe way: show what will change, get a yes, repair the links and say where it was saved. Remind them they can ask for this any time, not only today.

When the tour ends, or the owner wants to stop, carry on with their first real job in the same session.
